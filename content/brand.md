## Industries Served

The firm's ideal clients are agriculture and ranching operations, restaurants, retail businesses, professional services firms and practices, real estate businesses and investors, and non-profit organizations, associations, foundations and charities. The two industries below have dedicated focus areas.

### Non-Profit Organizations

**Who:** Associations, foundations, charities and other service organizations.

**Value proposition**
- **Compliance without the stress.** The firm prepares Form 990 and 990-T and keeps clients compliant with state law, so tax-exempt status is never at risk.
- **Financials your board can understand.** The firm walks board and staff through the financial statements and explains what the numbers mean.
- **Audit-ready and funder-ready.** Annual audits and reviewed or compiled statements are prepared to meet the requirements of funders and the state.

**Pain points**
- Form 990 and 990-T filing is complex, and errors or late filings risk tax-exempt status.
- Board members and funders expect clear, timely financial statements, but staff often lack accounting expertise.
- Grant and restricted-fund accounting is hard to track and report.
- Audit, review or compilation requirements vary by funder and state.
- State law compliance and registration deadlines are easy to miss with small or volunteer-run teams.
- Limited budgets make full-time finance staff hard to justify.
- 501(c)(3) setup and Form 1023 are intimidating for new organizations.

**Services:** annual audits, quarterly and annual financial statements, reviewed or compiled statements, Forms 990 and 990-T, supporting schedules, state law compliance, and 501(c)(3) applications including Form 1023. Form 990 preparation and exempt-organization compliance is a confirmed focus area.

**Typical triggers:** a funder requires an audit, a filing is missed or late, the board turns over, or a bookkeeper or finance staff member leaves.

### Real Estate

**Who:** Real estate businesses and investors, including agents, brokers, portfolio investors and flippers.

**Value proposition**
- **Tax planning before the deal.** The firm models the tax impact of buying, flipping, holding or exchanging a property before the client commits, so there are no surprises at closing.
- **Real estate-specific tax strategy.** This covers depreciation, 1031 exchanges, entity structure and deductions for agents, brokers and investors.
- **Clean books by property.** Property-level bookkeeping and financial reports satisfy lenders and partners.

**Pain points: agents and brokers**
- Irregular commission-based 1099 income makes estimated taxes hard to plan.
- Business expenses such as mileage, marketing, licensing and home office are poorly tracked and under-deducted.
- They are unsure whether an S-corp or LLC structure would save money.
- Business and personal finances are not separated, so books are messy.
- Cash flow swings with market and closing timelines.

**Pain points: portfolio investors and flippers**
- Buy, flip and hold strategies are taxed differently (capital gains vs. ordinary income, dealer status).
- Depreciation, cost segregation and passive loss rules are confusing.
- 1031 exchange timing and rules are strict and costly to get wrong.
- Property-level books are hard to keep separate across properties and entities.
- Entity structure and liability protection get messy as a portfolio grows.
- Lenders and partners want clean property-level financials.
- Tax liability is hard to forecast around sales.

**Typical triggers**
- A big sale or flip closes and brings an unexpected tax bill.
- A second or third property is purchased, or the portfolio outgrows current bookkeeping.
- An agent's or broker's income jumps, or they move from W-2 to 1099 and want to know if an S-corp or LLC would help.
- A 1031 exchange or property sale is coming and they need guidance on timing.
- Their current preparer misses deductions or depreciation, or doesn't respond.
- A lender or partner asks for clean property-level financials.
