## LinkedIn

A Laramie non-profit holds a $500,000 reserve at 60 percent stocks and 40 percent bonds. Stocks rise 25 percent, and the mix quietly becomes 65/35. Nobody on the board voted for that.

Then stocks fall 30 percent. The drifted fund loses about 19.6 percent, while the rebalanced one loses 18 percent. That is a gap of roughly $9,000, and one $30,000 stock sale would have closed it.

The fix is a routine a volunteer board can keep: match each pool of money to when it will be needed, review quarterly, and rebalance when any bucket drifts more than 5 points from target.

Also check new holdings. A fund that passes through K-1 income can trigger Form 990-T once gross unrelated business income reaches $1,000. We prepare Form 990 and 990-T, so send us the fund structure before the money goes in.
