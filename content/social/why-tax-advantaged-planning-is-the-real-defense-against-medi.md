## LinkedIn

A $12,000 hospital bill can drain a healthy savings cushion fast, even for a Laramie nonprofit director with family coverage and six months of operating reserves.

The part people miss: savings are after-tax money. Paying a $4,000 medical bill from checking takes about $5,690 of gross pay. Paying it from an HSA funded through a paycheck deduction takes $4,000. That is roughly $1,690 you never had to earn.

Nonprofits control more of this than they think. A cafeteria plan lets staff fund HSAs and flexible spending accounts with pre-tax dollars. A $500 employer HSA contribution for eight employees is a $4,000 budget line, and it is a real answer when someone asks what the organization does about health costs. Megan Hanneman, CGMA, works with management teams to build numbers like these into the forecast before a crisis, not after.

The full post also covers the 7.5% of AGI floor on the medical expense deduction and the FSA deadline rules.
