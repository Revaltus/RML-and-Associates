## LinkedIn

Six extra nights at a Snowy Range cabin cost one Laramie couple an $8,600 deduction.

The example from our new post: they rent the cabin 60 nights at $200, so $12,000 of income. With 14 personal nights, it counts as rental property, and about $20,600 of expenses (depreciation included) create a paper loss of roughly $8,600. At 20 personal nights, it becomes a residence with rental use. Deductions stop at rental income, and the loss disappears.

The line is the greater of 14 days or 10% of rental days. Rent for fewer than 15 days and the income is tax-free.

Nonprofit leaders, this one reaches you too. A cabin week donated to a gala auction counts as personal use, and you generally can't deduct its value. One auction item can push you over the line.

Count your days before the year starts, not in March. Full worked example here: https://rmlcpas.com/resources/how-to-turn-a-second-home-into-a-tax-win
