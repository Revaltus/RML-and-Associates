## LinkedIn

200 hours at a food pantry. $0 deduction. The IRS does not let you write off the value of your time, no matter what you would normally bill for it.

What it does allow is unreimbursed, out-of-pocket costs for a qualified charity: supplies, parking, overnight travel and mileage at 14 cents per mile. Congress sets that rate, so it does not move with gas prices. Picture a Laramie board treasurer who drives to Cheyenne eight times, adds 1,500 local miles for a food pantry and buys $90 in supplies. That lands near $490 in deductions.

Two catches. You have to itemize on Schedule A, and at $250 or more for one organization you need a written acknowledgment from it before you file. Ask in January, not April.

If you sit on a Laramie non-profit board, a one-page acknowledgment letter template takes about ten minutes to set up and saves your treasurer a winter of one-off requests.
