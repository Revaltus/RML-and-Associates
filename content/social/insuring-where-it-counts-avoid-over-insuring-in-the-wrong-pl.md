## LinkedIn

A Laramie youth-services nonprofit with $800,000 in annual expenses and $200,000 in unrestricted reserves can likely absorb an $8,000 to $16,000 loss without cutting a program. So why is it paying extra to keep a $500 deductible?

Most boards never ask. At renewal, the treasurer compares this year's premium to last year's and wonders if it can come down. The better question is which losses your reserves could not absorb.

Our new post gives a simple starting point: set your retention at roughly 1 to 2 percent of annual operating expenses, count only unrestricted net assets, and put premium dollars toward losses above that line. It also covers where gaps hide, including employee theft, D&O, donor data and fundraising events. And it explains why landlord and funder contract minimums come before any cut.

Megan Hanneman, CGMA, works with management teams on budgeting and cost analysis. She can help you model what a retained loss would do to monthly cash, so your talk with the agent starts from your numbers.
