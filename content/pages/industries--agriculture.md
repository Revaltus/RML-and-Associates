## How can drought deferrals and income timing protect your cash?

A dry summer can force a hard choice: sell part of the herd early, or buy feed you can't afford. Either way, selling more cattle than usual in one year can push your income into a higher bracket and leave you with a tax bill on money you were forced to raise.

Federal rules may let ranchers who sell livestock because of drought postpone reporting some of that extra income, generally for a year, so it lands closer to when you rebuild the herd. Whether you qualify depends on your numbers and on drought conditions in your area. Every case gets a CPA's review before you rely on it.

That's why timing matters. Looking ahead on taxes means talking before you sell, not after. We look at what you expect to sell, what you plan to replace and how the brackets stack up, so you can plan with confidence. Nobody can promise a specific savings figure, but you will know your options while there is still time to use them.

The best moment for this conversation is the first time you think, "We might have to thin the herd." Call then.

## How does succession planning keep the ranch in the family?

Most ranch families share the same unspoken question: how does this land and this operation reach the next generation without a tax shock? Waiting until health or markets force the answer is the expensive way to find out.

The tax side of a transfer depends on how it happens. A buy-in for a son or daughter who is coming home, a gradual gift of ownership interests, a sale to family and a lease-back each produce different results. Timing a transition around a strong or weak income year changes the math too. RML models those paths so you can compare them before anything is signed.

Sherrie McKinney brings experience from BDO USA's Private Client Services division to these conversations about family finances and handoffs. You sit down with the people who will still be here when the transition happens. Our [advisory services](/advisory) are built for exactly this kind of long view.
