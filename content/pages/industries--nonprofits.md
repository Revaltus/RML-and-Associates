## Compliance without the stress for nonprofits

If you run a nonprofit, you probably carry the filing risk without a finance background. Maybe you are the executive director who also answers the phones, or a board treasurer who volunteered because someone had to. That is a lot to handle, especially when one late or incorrect return can put your tax-exempt status in question.

RML and Associates CPAs is a firm in Laramie, and accounting for nonprofits in Wyoming, Nebraska and Colorado is a core part of our work. We prepare your Form 990 and 990-T and keep you compliant with state law, so the mission for your organization, association, foundation or charity stays the focus.

## Form 990 and 990-T preparation that protects your tax-exempt status

Form 990 is not a form you want to learn by trial and error. It is long and detailed, and a mistake or a missed deadline can put your tax-exempt status at risk. The 990-T adds another layer when your organization earns unrelated business income. Our job is to take that off your plate and get it right.

For non-profit tax preparation in and Form 990 preparation, here is what is included:

- Annual Form 990 preparation and filing for your 501(c)(3) or other exempt organization
- Form 990-T preparation when unrelated business income needs to be reported
- Deadline tracking, so you hear from us before a due date, not after
- State compliance, including the filings Wyoming expects from nonprofit corporations
- A plain-language review of the return before it goes out, so you know what you are signing

What do you send us? Last year's return if you have it, your year-end financial records, and a short note on anything that changed: new programs, new revenue sources, a large grant. We will ask follow-up questions if we need them. That is the whole routine.

## Financial statements your board and funders can understand

Board members and funders expect clear, accurate reporting. Many nonprofits are staffed by volunteers or a very small team, and nobody has time to translate a spreadsheet into something a board can act on.

We support your day-to-day nonprofit accounting and keep your records consistent from month to month. Then we turn them into financial statements that a board member without a finance background can read. You see what came in, what went out, and where restricted funds stand. If you need nonprofit financial statements that hold up in a board meeting, this is where we start.

Board turnover is a common moment to call. A new treasurer should not have to reverse-engineer the last one's system. We give them a clean, consistent picture from the first meeting, and we are available by phone when questions come up.

## Getting and keeping your tax-exempt status

Starting a nonprofit means more than choosing a name. The tax-exemption application sets the foundation for everything that follows, including the Form 990 you will file each year and the state requirements you will need to keep up with. Get the application right, and later filings are far easier. Miss a filing down the road, and that early work can be undone.

Whether you are applying for 501(c)(3) status for the first time or tidying up an established organization, we help with the nonprofit tax exemption application in Wyoming and the ongoing upkeep that goes with it. That includes Wyoming, Nebraska and Colorado non-profit corporation filing, so nothing slips through between the state and the IRS.

Before our first call, it helps to gather:

- Your organization's name, mission and a short description of its activities
- Your articles of incorporation and bylaws, if they exist
- A list of current or planned board members
- Any past IRS or state correspondence

Do not worry if you do not have all of it. We will sort out what is missing together.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Nonprofits

**Q: What does a CPA do for a nonprofit?**
A: A CPA prepares your Form 990 and, when needed, Form 990-T, keeps your records consistent, and produces financial statements your board can read. We also track filing deadlines and state compliance so your tax-exempt status stays protected, and we answer questions in plain English.

**Q: What is the difference between Form 990 and Form 990-T?**
A: Form 990 is the annual information return most tax-exempt organizations file to report finances and activities. Form 990-T reports unrelated business income, meaning earnings from activities outside your exempt purpose. Some organizations file both. We review your revenue sources and tell you which returns apply.

**Q: What happens if a nonprofit files its Form 990 late?**
A: Late or incorrect filings can put your tax-exempt status at risk, so act quickly. Call us as soon as you realize a deadline has passed. We will review what was missed, help you get the return prepared and filed, and set up deadline tracking so it does not happen again.

**Q: Can you help if a funder requires an audit?**
A: Yes. We help you understand what the funder is asking for, get your records audit-ready, and guide you through the process. Whether we can serve as the auditor depends on your situation, and we will tell you plainly what we can and cannot do.

**Q: What should I gather before applying for 501(c)(3) status?**
A: Bring your organization's name, a short description of its mission and activities, your articles of incorporation and bylaws if you have them, a list of current or planned board members, and any past IRS or state correspondence. Missing pieces are fine. We will sort them out on the first call.

**Q: Do you offer a free consultation for nonprofits?**
