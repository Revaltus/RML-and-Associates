## Commission income without the tax surprise

A closing lands in March, nothing in April, and two in May. If you're a real estate agent or broker paid on commission, you know the rhythm. The IRS still expects estimated tax payments on a schedule, whether or not your income follows one.

We set your estimated payments around your actual closings and pending pipeline, not last year's total divided by four. When a big commission arrives, we adjust. When a slow stretch hits, we adjust again, so you aren't sending money you may need for marketing or rent.

Then there are the expenses. Mileage to showings, signs and photography, licensing and MLS fees, continuing education, the home office where you write offers at 9 p.m. These costs go untracked and under-deducted because nobody wrote them down when they happened. We show you what to record, and we make sure the legitimate ones reach your return. If you need a real estate agent tax accountant in Wyoming, Nebraska or Colorado who starts with your calendar of closings, you want RML and Associates.

## Know the tax impact before you close

The call usually comes after the closing. A flip sold, a building traded, a long-held lot finally under contract, and then a tax bill nobody modeled. By then most of the choices are already made.

We run the numbers first. Before you commit, we model the tax result of buying, flipping, holding or exchanging a property, side by side, using your real income and your basis in the property. You see what each path leaves you after federal and state tax, and you decide with that in hand.

A 1031 exchange belongs in that comparison when you're selling investment real estate. Done correctly, it defers capital gains tax by rolling the proceeds into like-kind property. It also brings hard deadlines, 45 days to identify a replacement and 180 days to close, and a qualified intermediary who must hold the funds. We lay it next to a straight sale and a long-term hold so you can see whether deferral beats cash in hand for your situation. Not every deal is a fit, and we'll tell you when it isn't.

If you're a portfolio investor or flipper weighing your next deal, bring us the numbers while there's still room to change the plan. That's the core of our 1031 exchange and investor work for Wyoming clients.

## Books that keep pace as your portfolio grows

Portfolios usually outgrow their records before their owners notice. A spreadsheet that worked for two rentals starts hiding problems at five: a late payment here, a repair coded to the wrong building there.

Good rental property bookkeeping in Laramie, WY comes down to two things. Rent and expenses tracked by property, and year-end numbers you can trust. When those are right, your tax return starts from accurate figures, and any planning around a purchase or sale does too.

Property managers and developers need one more layer: each property or project kept separate, so one building's repairs or one project's costs never blur into another's. That is the heart of property management accounting. ## Frequently Asked Questions About Real estate

**Q: How do real estate agents handle estimated taxes on commission income?**
A: Agents paid on 1099 commission generally owe federal estimated payments each quarter. Instead of splitting last year's total into four, we base payments on your closed deals and pending pipeline, then adjust after big commissions or slow stretches. That keeps you from overpaying in thin months or facing a bill in April.

**Q: What is a 1031 exchange and when does it make sense?**
A: A 1031 exchange lets an investor defer capital gains tax on the sale of investment real estate by reinvesting the proceeds in like-kind property. The rules are strict: you must identify a replacement within 45 days and close within 180, using a qualified intermediary. We compare it against selling outright before you list.

**Q: What do landlords need for their tax return?**
A: Landlords need rent collected, repairs, insurance, mortgage interest, and property taxes tracked by property, plus purchase details so depreciation can be calculated for each building. With clean year-end numbers, we prepare accurate federal and state returns and show how each added property changes your result.

**Q: Which expenses should a real estate agent track?**
A: Common categories include mileage to showings, marketing and signage, licensing and MLS fees, continuing education, and a qualifying home office. Deductibility depends on your facts, so we review your records and tell you what to track going forward. Tracking them as they happen matters more than any single category.

**Q: Do you work with real estate clients outside Laramie?**
A: Yes. RML is based in Laramie and serves clients across Wyoming, Nebraska and Colorado. If your rentals or deals cross state lines, we prepare the federal and state returns together so nothing is missed.
