## Why restaurant finances are hard to read

A packed Saturday night doesn't mean the restaurant made money. Margins are thin, a lot of the day's take arrives as cash, tipped staff add reporting rules, and sales tax is collected on every ticket. Put those together and the real result gets buried.

Most restaurant owners we talk with are busy, proud of the food, and unsure what the year actually earned until the return is done. That is a hard way to run a business you built yourself.

RML and Associates CPAs is the restaurant accountant in Laramie, WY who restaurant owners call when they want straight answers. For independent restaurants and every kind of food service business, we look ahead so you can plan with confidence.

## Restaurant tip credits you should not leave unclaimed

If your servers, bartenders or other staff earn tips, your restaurant may qualify for a federal tax credit tied to the employer payroll taxes you pay on those tips. It is one of the most commonly missed credits in the industry, mostly because it lives in the gap between payroll and the business return.

Here is how we handle it. We start by reviewing how tips are reported and how payroll taxes on them are being calculated. Then we work out whether the credit applies, and claim it on your business return when it does. Because we also look after state and federal payroll tax, the numbers come from one set of hands instead of being passed between a payroll provider and a preparer.

We won't promise a figure before we've seen your records. What we can do is check. Ask about tip credits during your free consultation and we'll tell you plainly whether there is something to claim.

## Monthly financials that show where the money goes

If you only learn the year's result when taxes are due, you can't fix anything in time. Current restaurant bookkeeping turns into profit and loss statements you can read each month. Food cost, labor cost and overhead sit side by side, period by period.

That is when a menu price that no longer covers the plate shows up, or when labor creeps past where it should be. You see it in March, not the following February.

We walk through the statements with you in plain language. If something looks off, you hear about it from us first. Accounting for restaurants across works best when the owner actually uses the numbers.

## Planning ahead for a second location, a buyout or a sale

The biggest restaurant decisions rarely arrive on a tax deadline. A new oven line, a second location, a partner who wants in, or an owner who is ready to step back: each one changes your tax picture, and each goes better when you've talked it through first.

That is why our conversations run all year, not just in filing season. Before you sign for major equipment, we look at timing and cash. When you're weighing a partner or a second location, we model what it does to your taxes and your cash flow. If you're thinking about succession, or about a merger, acquisition or outright sale, we help you map the next three to five years so you aren't deciding under pressure.

You know the kitchen and the guests. We bring the planning for the money side, so the decision is yours and well informed.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Restaurants

**Q: What is the restaurant tip credit?**
A: It is a federal tax credit for restaurants with tipped employees, tied to the employer payroll taxes paid on those tips. It is often missed because it sits between payroll and the business return. We review your tip reporting and claim the credit on your return when you qualify.

**Q: Do you help with restaurant sales tax in Wyoming?**
A: Yes. We prepare and file local and state sales tax returns and track the due dates for you. If your restaurant also sells or caters in Nebraska or Colorado, we keep those requirements on the same calendar so no filing slips through.

**Q: Can you handle both my business and personal tax returns?**
A: Yes. We prepare state and federal income tax returns for your restaurant and can prepare your personal return too. Looking at both together shows how business decisions affect your own tax bill, which makes it easier to plan ahead.

**Q: What should I bring to the free consultation?**
A: Bring last year's tax return and a recent profit and loss statement. With those two documents we can see where your restaurant stands, check for missed credits like the tip credit, and suggest what to work on first. You'll leave with a clear next step.

**Q: Can you help if I want to open another location or sell?**
A: Yes. We talk through equipment purchases, a second location, bringing in a partner and succession before you commit. We look at how each choice affects your taxes and cash flow, so you can decide what to do over the next three to five years with confidence.
